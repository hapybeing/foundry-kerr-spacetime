THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS OF KERR SPACETIME

1. The Kerr Metric Tensor

A stationary, axisymmetric, uncharged rotating black hole of mass $M$ and angular momentum $J = aM$ is uniquely described in general relativity by the Kerr metric (Roy Kerr, 1963). In standard Boyer-Lindquist coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$ with $G = c = M = 1$:

$$ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2r}{\rho^2}\right) dt^2 - \frac{4ar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2} dt\,d\phi + \frac{\rho^2}{\Delta} dr^2 + \rho^2 d\theta^2 + \left(r^2 + a^2 + \frac{2a^2 r\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\right)\sin^2\theta d\phi^2$$

where the structural scalars are defined as:

$$\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 \cos^2\theta, \quad \Delta = r^2 - 2r + a^2$$

2. Kerr-Schild Cartesian Formulation for GPU Raytracing

To eliminate the coordinate singularity at the outer event horizon $r = r_+$ and avoid polar coordinate axis artifacts ($\theta = 0, \pi$), our engine formulates light ray propagation in Cartesian Kerr-Schild coordinates $x^\mu = (t, x, y, z)$:

$$g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + f(x, y, z) k_\mu k_\nu, \quad f = \frac{2 M r^3}{r^4 + a^2 z^2}$$

where $k_\mu$ is a null vector in Minkowski spacetime ($\eta^{\mu\nu} k_\mu k_\nu = 0$). By the Sherman-Morrison theorem, the inverse metric is exact with zero matrix inversion:

$$g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - f(x, y, z) k^\mu k^\nu$$

Hamilton's equations for null geodesics ($H = \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu\nu} p_\mu p_\nu = 0$) are integrated via a symplectic Verlet integrator with closed-form analytic gradient $\nabla_{\vec{x}} H$.

3. Horizons, Ergosphere & Frame-Dragging

The event horizons occur at the coordinate roots $\Delta = 0$:

$$r_\pm = M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$$

The static limit surface (outer boundary of the ergosphere) occurs where the asymptotic time-translation Killing vector becomes null ($g_{00} = 0$):

$$r_E(\theta) = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2 \cos^2\theta}$$

Inside the ergosphere ($r_+ < r < r_E$), the frame-dragging effect forces all physical observers to rotate with the spacetime geometry at angular velocity $\omega = -g_{t\phi}/g_{\phi\phi}$.

4. Relativistic Doppler Beaming & Gravitational Redshift

Gas elements in the thin Shakura-Sunyaev accretion disk orbit on prograde circular equatorial Keplerian trajectories with angular velocity $\Omega_K = 1 / (r^{3/2} + a)$. The frequency shift factor $g$ is:

$$g = \frac{\nu_{\text{obs}}}{\nu_{\text{em}}} = \frac{1}{u^t_{\text{em}} (1 - \Omega_K L_z)}$$

By Liouville's theorem in curved spacetime, the specific intensity transforms as $I_{\text{obs}}(\nu) = g^3 I_{\text{em}}(\nu/g)$. Integrated over bolometric bandwidth, the observed radiance scales as:

$$I_{\text{obs}} = g^4 I_{\text{em}}$$

This $g^4$ factor produces the striking relativistic asymmetry: the side approaching the observer is blindingly bright and blueshifted, while the receding side is dimmed and redshifted.

5. The Penrose Process & Black Hole Energetics

Inside the ergosphere, the timelike Killing vector $\xi^\mu = (\partial_t)^\mu$ becomes spacelike ($g_{00} > 0$), allowing test particles to have negative energy relative to an observer at infinity: $E = -p_t < 0$.

Roger Penrose (1969) proved that if a parent particle with energy $E_0$ enters the ergosphere and decays into two fragments:

$$E_0 = E_1 + E_2$$

If Fragment 1 is injected onto a negative-energy retrograde orbit ($E_1 < 0$) and falls into the black hole horizon, Fragment 2 escapes to infinity with energy:

$$E_2 = E_0 - E_1 > E_0$$

The infalling negative-energy particle decreases the mass and angular momentum of the black hole, extracting up to 20.7% of the particle's rest mass in a single fission event, and up to 29.29% of the black hole's entire mass via the Christodoulou reducible energy formula:

$$M_{\text{irr}} = \sqrt{\frac{r_+^2 + a^2}{2}}, \quad E_{\text{rot}} = M - M_{\text{irr}}$$
